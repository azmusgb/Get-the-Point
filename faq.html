PLAYING THE GAME

THE CORE LOOP

What is DECISIONS, DECISIONS?

A social party game built around choice under time pressure. Each prompt gives the active player three ways to play with different point values. Weigh the tradeoff, choose one, start clueing, and get your teammates to guess before time runs out.

Is this just Pictionary, charades, and humming in one box?

No. HUM, DRAW, and MIME are routes for communicating the same prompt. The core mechanic is the decision before performance: one prompt, prompt-specific point values, and the player chooses which route is worth it.

What makes a good prompt?

A strong prompt is representable, clear, fair for the intended audience, and creates decision tension: once the points are considered, at least two routes should feel plausibly attractive instead of one route being obvious every time.

How does scoring work?

Every prompt has its own point values. No method is permanently worth more. If your team guesses correctly before the turn ends, you score the points attached to the route you chose.

Can I switch after I start?

No. Once clueing begins, your choice is locked for that prompt. In the digital prototype, one tap chooses and commits the method; there is no second confirmation screen.

What are the current methods?

The current challenger uses HUM → DRAW → MIME. Those labels, order, colors, and final visual treatment are still being tested.

What does each method allow?

HUM: melody or humming only, with no spoken words or lyrics. DRAW: sketch the prompt with no words, letters, or numbers. MIME: use movement, gestures, and facial expression without speaking.

CURRENT PROTOTYPE

WHAT IS STILL BEING TESTED

What is the timer?

The current baseline uses 60-second, 30-second, and 15-second rounds, plus a 15-second tiebreak condition. Those are playtest defaults, not final commercial rules.

Can players pass?

Pass behavior is still being tested. Some sessions use no voluntary pass; others test a limited emergency pass. The final rule is not locked.

When does the timer start?

The current digital prototype can test different timer-start behavior. Whether the final timer begins on reveal or on the player's choice remains open.

Does the browser demo use my camera or microphone?

No. Core play does not require camera or microphone recording. Game state stays local on the device unless a tester explicitly exports data or submits feedback.

Can I play the current demo?

The full browser build is a private playtest tool. Invited testers can enter the private demo. Everyone else can try the public prompt example or join a playtest.

PHYSICAL + DIGITAL

PRODUCT DIRECTION

What will the physical game include?

Current prototype direction is approximately 100 double-sided cards, a reusable drawing surface, dry-erase marker, quick-start rules, simple scoring, and a phone timer. Final materials and package specifications still require supplier validation.

Is there an app?

There is a working browser playtest prototype now. The preferred native direction is iPhone-first, offline, pass-and-play, with local content, minimal permissions, and no unnecessary backend.

How will new content work?

Prompts are the expansion engine. New packs should add fresh situations and choices without creating new core mechanics.

Will there be a subscription?

Not by default. The preferred model is a low-friction starter, a one-time Core unlock, optional one-time theme packs, and a separate physical edition.

PROJECT STATUS

WHAT IS LOCKED — AND WHAT IS NOT

Is DECISIONS, DECISIONS the final commercial name?

No. DECISIONS, DECISIONS is the current lead working commercial title. The product team has chosen it as the active brand direction, but commercial use still requires legal validation, including trademark/common-law and relevant marketplace clearance. The earlier GET THE POINT naming work remains historical evidence and a fallback benchmark, not current naming authority.

Why does the name fit the mechanic?

Because the game repeatedly creates a small decision before performance: see the prompt, compare the route-specific points, choose, commit, then try to make the team guess. The name works only if the prompts actually create meaningful tradeoffs, which is why decision tension is now part of content review and playtesting.

When will it launch?

There is no locked launch date. The game should first prove itself in blind playtesting, then move through legal validation, manufacturing, pricing, and launch-readiness work.

What are you trying to prove right now?

The key milestone is simple: strangers understand the game, laugh, make meaningful choices, and want to play again.
