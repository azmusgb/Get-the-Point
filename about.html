DECISIONS, DECISIONS
ABOUT THE GAME

THE INTERESTING PART HAPPENS BEFORE THE CLUE.

DECISIONS, DECISIONS is a social party game where every prompt gives you three ways to play. Each route is worth different points, so the first challenge is deciding which way is worth it.

THE CORE IDEA

CHOICE UNDER TIME PRESSURE.

Most communication games tell you how to give the clue. DECISIONS, DECISIONS makes that choice part of the game. See the prompt, compare the points, pick a route, commit, then get your team to guess before time runs out.

One prompt. Three ways to play.

Different point values create the tradeoff. The best route should not always be the easiest one.

WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

THE CHOICE HAS TO FEEL REAL.

If one route is always obvious, the prompt is not doing enough work.

WEIGH

Compare the tradeoff.

Look at the same prompt three different ways. The points tell you what each route is worth right now.

COMMIT

Pick your move.

Choose the route you want and start clueing. Once performance begins, you do not switch.

PAYOFF

Make the risk matter.

A harder route can earn more, but only if your team gets the answer before the clock runs out.

CONTENT STANDARD

GOOD PROMPTS CREATE DECISION TENSION.

A strong prompt is not merely representable. At least two routes should feel plausibly attractive once the point values are considered.

Playtesting tracks what players choose, how long they take to choose, whether the route succeeds, how quickly teammates guess, and which prompts feel dead or obvious. Weak prompts get revised or removed instead of padding the deck.

PHYSICAL + DIGITAL

SAME DECISION. TWO FORMATS.

The format can change without changing what makes the game work.

PHYSICAL

Made for the table.

Prompt cards, a reusable drawing surface, quick-start rules, and simple scoring keep the attention on the people playing.

DIGITAL

Made to stay out of the way.

The digital direction is fast pass-and-play: reveal the prompt, choose, time the turn, score, and hand it off.

PLAYTESTING NOW

THE GAME HAS TO WORK WITHOUT US IN THE ROOM.

The next proof is simple: new groups understand it quickly, make meaningful choices, laugh, and want another round.
